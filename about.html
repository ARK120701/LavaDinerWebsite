Our Story

Why a diner on Hillside Ave is named after a volcano, and why we still cook the way we did on day one.

The name comes from the griddle

Ask anyone who's watched a burger hit that flat-top at 6am and they'll understand. We wanted a name as direct as the food: hot, fast, and a little stubborn about doing things the same way every day. So: Lava Diner. Not a gimmick — just what the kitchen actually looks like at rush hour.

We opened on Hillside Ave to fill a specific gap: a counter where the eggs are cracked to order, the coffee never sits, and nobody's rushing you out of a booth. That's still the whole plan.

Cooked to order, every time

Nothing sits under a heat lamp longer than it has to. Eggs go on when you order them, burgers get pressed when yours is called, and the pie case gets refilled every morning — not restocked from a truck. It's slower than a drive-through and we're fine with that.

New Hyde Park is a commuter town, so we open early and keep the griddle on late. Whoever's behind the counter at 7am knows the regulars' orders before they sit down, and that's the kind of place we're trying to stay.
